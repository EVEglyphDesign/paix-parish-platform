Why you specifically. Every Lenexa SRO is NASRO-certified, and during the school year SROs are assigned full-time into their buildings — then run the summer Youth Police Academy. That makes the School Resource Unit the one part of the department that is already, formally, in the education business. A stewardship framework for teaching material belongs with you, not with a procurement office.

Where this lands in your job

SROs “fulfill three basic functions in their school assignments”: law enforcement officer, informal counselor, and guest teacher — for instruction in law-related education, violence prevention and de-escalation, internet safety, bullying, alcohol and drug prevention, and crime prevention.

Lenexa Police Department, Police Department Structure

Internet safety and bullying prevention are already on that list. Both are subjects where the teaching material itself collects, or is tempted to collect, information about the children being taught. That is the exact point where a copyright and stewardship framework stops being paperwork and starts being a safeguard. What ARK actually is

ARK is the authorship and stewardship block that sits at the foot of every page of every EVE Glyph Design surface — 660 pages across thirteen parishes and nine languages today. It is one paragraph, and it is deliberately short enough that a department's counsel can read it in under a minute. It does two things: it reserves the design work, and it names who holds the right to assign that work to a large public institution.

What an institution receives

  • A governance receipt — a written record of what the institution was granted and on what terms.
  • Exit at any time, with a full export of its own content. No hostage data, no migration penalty.
  • Material it can teach and publish under its own name, with the authorship line intact at the foot of the page.

Stewardship of rights of use and assignment for large public and institutional usage rests with the Pacific Utilities Design Council. For a municipal department that is the operative sentence: it identifies a standing body as the counterparty for any assignment at institutional scale, rather than leaving a city to contract with one individual and hope for continuity.

The doctrine, before the pitch

No profiling of individuals

No person is counted, categorised or targeted by this platform. Ever. On a school surface that means no student analytics, no engagement scoring, no behavioural flags, no cohort segmentation — nothing that produces a record about a named child.

Safety first, betterment second

The safety of content and identity comes before growth, engagement or profit. Where the two conflict, safety wins and the feature does not ship. Revenue ranks below both.

Institutional sovereignty

The institution binds the platform, not the other way round. It receives a governance receipt and may leave at any time with everything it put in.

No tracking on the surfaces

The live parish surfaces carry no cookies, no analytics and no third-party scripts other than a web font. That is verifiable by viewing source, not a promise in a privacy policy.

How it sits against the law you already work inside

This is orientation, not legal advice, and the city's own counsel should check every line of it. Sources are linked so they can.

How the ARK framework sits against existing law, by authority
AuthorityWhat it governsWhy the no-profiling rule matters here
FERPA
20 U.S.C. §1232g		 Records held by a school. Law-enforcement-unit records are carved out, but an SRO only reaches actual education records without consent if formally designated a school official under the Department of Education's criteria (ED guidance). Material that generates no student record cannot drag an officer across that line by accident.
PPRA
20 U.S.C. §1232h		 Parental rights over instructional material, and consent for surveys touching sensitive subjects (ED). Curriculum with a public, readable licence and no embedded survey instrument is straightforward to show a parent.
COPPA Children under 13. A school may consent for an ed-tech vendor only inside a narrow educational context — never for commercial or profiling use (FTC FAQ). Collecting nothing removes the question rather than answering it.
Kansas juvenile records
K.S.A. 38-2309 & 38-2310		 Access to juvenile records, with restrictions for children under 14 and carve-outs letting educators see what is necessary for campus safety (38-2309 and 38-2310, Kansas Revisor). A programme that creates no juvenile record creates no juvenile record to fight over.
KORA
K.S.A. 45-215 et seq.		 Kansas Open Records Act. Police records are presumptively open, with enumerated exemptions (Lenexa PD request process). Curriculum the department holds is not automatically shielded from a records request. Material that is already public by design has nothing to disclose under pressure.
Copyright
17 U.S.C. §105		 Federal works are public domain; state and municipal works generally are not, and remain copyrightable (Copyright Office Compendium; Georgia v. Public.Resource.Org, 2020). A city can hold and use licensed material without the authorship being erased into the public domain. Both sides keep what is theirs.

Why a framework adopted in Lenexa travels

American policing is not ten thousand unconnected departments. It is ten thousand departments wired to the same small set of standards bodies — which is precisely why a governance framework proven in one city is legible in the next one.

StandardsNASRO

Certifies every Lenexa SRO. Its own curriculum is fully copyrighted, all rights reserved — so a designer retaining copyright while licensing to a department is the norm in this field, not an unusual ask.

LeadershipIACP

Chief Eric Schmitz is a member (city announcement). Lenexa's former chief Ellen Hanson served on its executive board.

ReportingNIBRS

The FBI's incident-based standard, plus the CJIS Security Policy, already impose a common data discipline across agencies.

AccreditationCALEA

The national accreditation route. Lenexa's Fire Department publicises its CFAI accreditation; no CALEA or KLEAP status is published for the police department — stated here as an open question, not a criticism.

What is not yet settled

Open questions, stated up front

  1. No precedent was found for a named private designer licensing curriculum to a United States municipal police department while retaining copyright. This would be new ground for both sides, and should be treated as such rather than presented as routine.
  2. Lenexa PD publishes no youth- or student-specific data policy. Nothing on lenexa.com addresses juvenile records handling, school data-sharing agreements or redaction practice for minors. That is a documented absence, not an allegation — and it is the gap a no-profiling framework is designed to fill.
  3. The SRO headcount is inconsistent between two official city pages, four against six. Worth correcting regardless of anything on this page.
  4. No department-level CALEA or KLEAP accreditation is published. NASRO certification of the SROs is the one standards affiliation stated plainly by the department.
  5. The city's own intellectual-property and procurement policy has not been reviewed here. Whatever is proposed has to survive it.

The ask

One conversation, no obligation

Not a purchase and not a pilot agreement. A reading of the ARK block by whoever in the city would have to live with it, and an honest answer on whether a designer-retained, institution-licensed model for school outreach material is something Lenexa could use — or whether it is not a fit. A clear no is a useful outcome.
